A tour · for the family

Save it when you see it. Find it when you sit down.

What callimachus is like to use, from the first tap to movie night. No settings, no typing, nothing to learn: you keep saving the way you already do.

01 · The problem

We save things everywhere, and then they vanish.

Each one meant "we should watch this". None of them can answer "what's a good film for an eight-year-old that we can stream tonight?"

02 · Save it the moment you see it

One tap, on your phone or your laptop.

Already saved

The old ones are found for you

The Chrome bookmark folders and Instagram saves you already have are read every morning, so years of "watch this someday" come in on their own.

03 · A library to browse, not a list to scroll

A page for every film, on every phone and laptop.

Each film you saved gets one page, with its poster, its ratings, what age it suits and where you can watch it. Then the library sorts them the ways a family actually asks.

Tonight

Watch tonight

What you can start right now on the services you pay for, or free. Or just what's under 90 minutes.

Together

Who's watching?

Pick who is on the couch and see only what suits the youngest in the room, that nobody has said no to.

Just me

My own list

Three ticks each: seen it, I wanna see it, and wait for me, so nobody watches the sequel without you.

Browse

Every way in

By genre, by decade, by the list it came from, or just what's very highly rated: IMDb 8 or more, or Rotten Tomatoes 90%.

One click

Everything about it

Every page links out to IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes, Letterboxd, Wikipedia, Common Sense Media and the rest.

One tap

Press play

Where to watch it, in order: on what you pay for first, then free, then rent. Videos and articles open straight to the page itself.

04 · Save from any phone or laptop

Save from all of them. Open it on all of them.

Every phone, iPad and laptop in the family can save, and every one opens the same library. A computer at home does the sorting quietly in between, so a film you save on the bus is in the library by the time you're home. A tick on one phone shows up on everyone's.

05 · Right for whoever's on the couch

The kids' phones get the kids' library.

Ages

What suits the room

Common Sense Media's age and its own sex and violence scores decide what a group can watch, not the rating alone.

Posters

Nothing explicit, ever

Every poster is checked at home before it reaches a phone. Anything that isn't clearly fine never appears.

Rules

The family decides

The family's own rules set what each view shows. A view only hides things; nothing anyone saved is ever deleted.

06 · Not only films

Whatever you meant to come back to.

Films and documentaries, series, short films, videos and talks, articles, books, podcasts and art.

Lists

A post of nine films

Becomes nine pages, each saying it's number 4 of 9, with the whole list one tap away.

Series

Save one, get the set

Save a post about a trilogy and all three films are filed, the one you saved marked.

Art

Something to see or buy

An artist's work, with where to see it or buy it.

If it guessed wrong

One tap fixes it

Nothing waits on you: its best guess is filed at once. If it picked the wrong film, choose the right one and the same page is fixed in place.

Nothing made up

A blank stays blank

Every rating and age comes from a public source and says which. If no source knows, the space stays empty rather than guessed.

It all stays at home
