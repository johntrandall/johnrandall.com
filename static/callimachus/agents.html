Under the hood · The agents

Agents only when something breaks, or could be better.

A healthy day starts no agent at all. When something does break, code (not an agent) decides that help is needed, a fresh agent session fixes the system rather than patching one note by hand, and the fix reaches John as one card he can say yes or no to.

01 · watchbill

A ship's roster for AI agents.

On a ship, the watch bill is the roster that says who stands which watch, who relieves whom, and who keeps the log. watchbill is the Claude Code plugin that does the same for agents: each one holds a named seat with a fixed brief, a pinned model and its own lane of files, and hands over with a written handoff instead of a fading memory.

Seats come in three write classes: those that change rules (the code and design), those that do work under the current rules, and those that change nothing and only read and report. A message from one agent to another is direction, never permission: only the person in the loop gives that.

Rules seat

Designer (build time)

Designs the system and lands changes with John's approval, only during build cycles. Dormant between them.

Work seats, run time

maintainer: repair and maintainer: observe

Fresh sessions callimachus launches itself, each from a fixed brief. They never change the design; they propose.

Crew

Readers, workers, verifiers

One step each, in a fresh context, then a report. A verifier checks work cold, without being told what to expect.

02 · Who may start, and what they may write

Every agent passes one gate.

The agent gate is the only way any agent starts. It reads the fuse first, then the session counts and the one-paid-session-at-a-time rule, and writes a gate record. A blown fuse means no agent, whoever is asking.

03 · The repair loop

What broke, and how do we stop it breaking?

After each run, code computes signals from the run's manifest: a source's response lost a field it always had (drift), a crash failed an item three runs running (defect), the share of saves finished without a person fell below its own four-week baseline (straight_through_low), John corrected a title (john_corrected). A signal opens once and stays open until a run shows it gone.

A signal, and only a signal, wakes maintainer: repair at 07:30: at most one session per signal per day, on Sonnet. If Sonnet cannot reproduce the failure or its fix fails the replay, the next day's attempt runs on Opus with Sonnet's report. If Opus fails too, the card goes to John marked "needs you", with both reports.

  1. Land what John approved. Any proposal he moved to yes is merged, its replay re-run on the merged result.
  2. Work around it, today, within the current rules: re-queue items, wait out a rate limit. Never edit a note by hand.
  3. Reproduce it offline. Nothing fetched is ever deleted, so the failing items replay from their saved responses without touching the network.
  4. Propose the fix on a branch: the code change, the failing case added to the regression suite, and a replay of the whole pipeline over every stored save.
  5. One pull request, one card. The card leads with what broke, the fix in one line, and the replay's counts, so John can decide without reading code.
  1. Check the records add up. Recount the run's outcomes with a script; every save must have one.
  2. Check outputs against their sources. Sample five filed notes and compare every field with its saved response. A value no source gave is a failure, raised at once.
  3. Look for improvements under six headings: accuracy, coverage, weight (calls, tokens, time), the views John uses, the repair loop itself, and the design.
  4. Propose, card by card, with the evidence, the expected effect and the cost to build. Never one that repeats a rejected idea: the rejection reasons are read first.
  5. Write the day's log page. A quiet day is one line.
04 · The observe loop

It works; how could it be better?

Repair answers to failures. maintainer: observe answers to the calendar: a daily watch at 08:00 while the build settles, reading records, never transcripts. It runs on Haiku, climbs to Sonnet only when Haiku cannot finish a check, and uses Opus only for a suggestion that would change the design.

"Settled" is measured in activity, not days: at least 30 runs in a row and 100 items, no new kind of fault, no failures, and the share of saves finished without a person holding at its own baseline. Then observe proposes going weekly; later, it proposes stopping. Both need John's yes.

05 · The model rule

Free or cheap, and never the biggest by default.

Always first

The local model

qwen3:30b on the home network's model box. If it is down, items wait for tomorrow; a missed day is fine.

Then, if unsure

Haiku

Only for an item the local model could not finish with confidence, within hourly and daily counts.

Only if absolutely needed

Sonnet

The top rung for naming a title, and the first rung for repairing the system.

Repair, last resort

Opus, never Fable

Opus before any person is asked. Fable is kept for designing, with John, never for running the system.

06 · Who says yes

Every decision is its own card.

Each proposal lands on a decisions board in the maintenance vault (the code's own folder, opened in Obsidian) as one card with full context and links, never bundled with another, and with no cap on how many may be open. The guardrails limit what agents do, never what John is shown.

John says what is correct (his corrections become expected answers in the regression suite), approves or rejects every change that does not land itself, undoes any change with one label, and is the only one who signs in to an account, resets the fuse or lifts a containment.

07 · Containment

Stop the smallest part that covers the risk.

When something risky is waiting for review, code holds just enough, in levels: park one fingerprint; hold one stage for one source; quarantine the notes that passed through it (hidden from every view by their quarantine field, never deleted); hold a whole source; blow the fuse; and, last, a full hold that stops every write. Narrowing is open to anyone; widening again is John's alone. Lifting one never has to restore anything, because nothing was destroyed.

Changes may one day land by themselves, but only in categories John enables, after shadow mode: the risk classifier judges every proposal from the first, while its verdict stays hidden on the card until John has given his own. Even then: at most two a week, a paired canary against the previous rules, and one-label undo.
