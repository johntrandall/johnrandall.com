Under the hood · How it works

Deterministic software, with AI only where it is needed.

Most "AI apps" put a model in charge and hope. callimachus does the opposite: plain code does every step it can, a free model on the home network handles the fuzzy part (naming a title from a caption), and anything more expensive has to climb a ladder, under counts, with a fuse that stops everything the moment the numbers look wrong.

01 · The pipeline

Eight stages, from a saved post to a note you can press play on.

One command, tools/callimachus daily, run by macOS's own scheduler at 06:30 for the places you save; the same stages run the moment a link is sent in. Every stage writes down what it did, so any run can be replayed later from what it saved.

1 · Capture

Every way in

Sent now: the Chrome button and right-click, the bookmarklet, and the phone's Share menu, all into a small private intake on the home network, filed at once. Read each morning: Chrome's bookmark folders (never written), Instagram's saved list (once a day, at a random hour), Instagram exports, the NYT's shared Saved list, the inbox notes. Each source reads newest first, down to its checkpoint.

2 · Ledger

Never analyse anything twice

One line per saved item: its identity (a post's shortcode, a bookmark's id), when it was first seen, and the verdict: media, not_media, unidentified, pending. A verdict is never redone by accident; unsaving a post changes nothing.

3 · Classify

Is it media, what kind, and what is it called?

Cheapest first: a URL on IMDb or Letterboxd is a film; then the caption, comments and link text; then text on screen, read on-device with Apple's Vision; then speech, transcribed by whisper.cpp. The kind: film, short, series, online video, article, book, podcast, list, or visual art. A title needs confidence 0.8 to file itself; below that it is proposed, with up to three candidates.

4 · Resolve

One title, one canonical id

Title, year and type become an id by deterministic matching: TMDb for film and TV, Open Library for books, the canonical URL for an article, the post itself for a work of art. Up to 20 titles come from one list post, each remembering its place; a named trilogy or series files every film in it, up to 12.

5 · Where to watch

One step to the thing itself

Films and series: TMDb's watch providers (data from JustWatch), on our services first, then other subscriptions, free, free with ads, rent or buy. Online video: the page's own player, else an archive.org copy. Articles: the page and its Wayback copy. Code checks every match (title, length, maker or year); an unsure copy is never made the link.

6 · Enrich

Facts from free sources

TMDb (credits, certifications, streaming via JustWatch, and the poster), OMDb (IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes, Metacritic, awards), Wikidata (the ids that link every site: Wikipedia, Letterboxd, Rotten Tomatoes, Metacritic, Common Sense), Common Sense Media (age, and its sex and violence scores, one page per title, never its search), Open Library (books). Paid tiers are never used.

7 · Check images new saves: soon

Nothing explicit reaches a phone

Every poster goes through NudeNet, an open-source nudity detector, on the CPU at home; an exposed-nudity label at 0.5 or above flags it. A flagged poster gets one yes-or-no question to a vision model on the model box (qwen3-vl). Only a clear no publishes it; anything else stays in the archive. Run once over the whole library: 729 posters, 2 flagged and both cleared, none blocked. No paid AI, and no image leaves the house.

8 · File

One note per title, at once

Into the callimachus vault, the one the family browses: frontmatter in a fixed order, a readable body, and a raw page per source. A best guess that passes the catalogue check is filed straight away, marked a guess; a later pick re-files the same note. A field no source gave stays blank; a field the schema does not list cannot be written.

And then · Review

Two questions per card

Is the record right, and can you watch, read or hear it in one step from the card? A judge reads every card first and sends a person only the ones it is unsure of. Each verdict is logged, and carries forward to an identical record.

02 · The escalation ladder

Climb one rung at a time.

"Escalate" does not mean "ask a person". It means hand the item one rung up: to an agent, then a smarter agent, then a paid one, then a smarter paid one, and only then to a person. An item climbs only when the rung below could not finish it with enough confidence, and every attempt is recorded: handler, model, tokens, result.

The same ladder serves two jobs: finishing an item (naming this title now) and fixing the system (stopping it from failing again). Naming a title tops out at Sonnet. Repairing the system may climb to Opus. Never further.

03 · Healthy, degraded, stopped

Always one of three states, computed by code.

The status note opens with the state of the last run. "Degraded" is not "broken": it is still producing correct notes, while working its way back to plain code.

Healthy

Every item finished by code or the free local model. The usual day. No paid call, no agent, nothing for anyone to do.

Degraded

Correct notes, but something ran above the deterministic path: Haiku named a title, or a source is on a fallback. An open signal drives a fix, and the condition is time-boxed at 14 days.

Stopped

A capability is held by the fuse, a containment or a time box. Capture keeps safely storing saves; whatever needs a model waits for a person.

04 · Breakers, caps and the fuse

A hard "something is wrong" switch.

Every failure gets a fingerprint: the kind of fault, the source, the stage, and the error with its numbers and ids stripped. Items with the same fingerprint are one problem, so one broken parser cannot launch fifty agents. A circuit breaker per fingerprint lets at most one repair start; the rest wait, parked, on the status note.

Caps are counts, never dollars: one attempt per rung per item, so many calls an hour per rung, one paid agent session per system at a time. Money is limited once, outside the code, by a spend limit on a key used for nothing else.

Above all of that sits the fuse. A breaker resets itself; a fuse blows and stays blown until a person replaces it, like the andon cord on a factory line. It is checked at the one choke point every model call and agent launch passes through.

The fuse blows on
TripCatches
more than 10 new fingerprints in a dayone problem showing up as many

Blown, it stops every model call and agent launch, local and paid. The deterministic parts keep running and keep storing saves. It tells John once, and only John can reset it, from his own terminal.

05 · The architecture

Drawn in C4, simplified.

C4 is Simon Brown's way of drawing software as maps at four zoom levels: the system in its world (level 1), the containers inside it, meaning the separately running programs and stores (level 2), their components (3) and code (4). These are redrawn from callimachus's own C4 model, with machine names replaced by roles and the smallest parts folded together.

07 · From the vault to the phones

Two vaults, one sync server, nothing in the cloud.

The engine (callimachus-engine: the code, the intake, the design and the review boards) and the library (callimachus: the notes the family reads) are separate Obsidian vaults and separate git repositories. Only the engine's tools write a record; the family adds their own ticks, and a correction to a note is picked up by the next run.

The views

Bases, not plugins

Nine .base files in three areas (Family, Collection, Person), 137 views, each named for its filter. Each person's three answers are checkboxes (seen_by_, want_to_see_, wait_for_); a missing field is not answered, so three states show natively. Formulas apply the family's rules, such as very highly rated: IMDb 8+ or Rotten Tomatoes 90%+.

The sync

Obsidian LiveSync, self-hosted

A CouchDB server on the home NAS, one database per vault, reachable only inside the family's private network over HTTPS. One login per person, each able to open only that database. Two-way: a change reached another vault in about two seconds when measured. Clashing edits to the same line are kept, never resolved by a machine.

The images

One poster, checked, then synced

One TMDb poster per title at width 342, kept as a fetch record. Only a passed image is copied to the vault's Posters/ folder and linked from poster; every note records poster_check (passed, blocked, unchecked), poster_checked_with and poster_checked_at. The card views show it as the cover.

Status, 27 October 2026: the views are built over the family's film and series notes, and Karina's iPhone is the first device live on the sync; every other family phone, iPad and Mac is next. The library has its posters (729, each checked), registry links (730 notes) and Common Sense scores (535 notes); new saves get links and scores now, and posters soon, when the engine's own check-images step is built.

08 · The way in

One tap from wherever you are.

Keep saving the way you already do, and callimachus reads those places each morning. Or send a thing the moment you find it: from the browser, from any phone's Share button, from Safari on the iPhone. Every way in ends at a small private intake on your own network, and the save is filed straight away.

In Chrome

Send to callimachus

A toolbar button sends the page you are on; a right-click sends a page or any link on it. A send never waits on the network: it queues in the browser and retries until the intake has it. Coming to the Chrome Web Store as an unlisted listing, so the family installs it in one click.

In any browser

A bookmarklet

One bookmark that sends the page you are on, in Safari on the Mac and the iPhone, or anywhere else. It carries no password: a small Add page asks once, and keeps it in that browser.

On the phone

Save to callimachus

In the Share menu of any app. It adds the link to a shared Reminders list, so the share closes at once, even with no signal; the family Mac files it as soon as it syncs, writes back what it filed, and ticks it off.

Already saved

Chrome bookmark folders

Intent folders such as Watch Me Someday and Read It Later, read every morning and never written to.

Already saved

Instagram

Posts and reels, from your saved list, read once a day at a quiet, random hour; and from an Instagram export, which is the only way anyone else's saves come in.

Already saved

The inbox

A folder of small notes: a link or a title to file, or a correction to a note already filed.

09 · Where to watch, checked by code

One step from the note to the thing itself.

A note is only useful if you can press play from it. So for every save callimachus looks for the quickest honest way to it, and checks the match with code, not a model: the title, the length and the maker or year must agree. A copy it is not sure of is shown as possible copy, not checked, and is never made the link.

Films and series

Streaming, in order

On the services we pay for first, then other subscriptions, free, free with ads, and rent or buy. US listings from TMDb, streaming data from JustWatch; a weekly refresh, so a list does not go stale, is planned.

Online video

The page, or the archive

The page's own player when it has one; otherwise a copy on archive.org, found by title and maker and checked for length.

Articles

The page, kept

The article itself, and the Wayback Machine's copy for the day the page moves or goes behind a wall.

Visual art

Where to see or buy it

The post it came from, the artist's shop or gallery page, or the artist's profile.

10 · Filed at once, corrected by you

Two questions on every card.

Each save gets a review card, and the card asks only two things. Is the record right? The kind, the title, the catalogue entry. Can you watch, read or hear it in one step from here? In the family's own words: the record is correct, and the media link, or the list of where it streams, “is consumable by human quickly and easily.”

A judge reads every card first and passes on only the ones it is unsure of. And nothing waits on you: when callimachus has a best guess that passes its catalogue check, it files it at once and marks it a guess. If you pick another candidate later, the same note is re-filed under your pick. Never a second note, and the run counts how many guesses were wrong.

11 · The library, in the weeds

A library to browse, not a list to scroll.

callimachus keeps two vaults. callimachus-engine does the work: the pipeline, the intake, the design and the review cards, and the family never needs to open it. What the family opens is the callimachus vault: one note per film or series, and Obsidian's own Bases over them, built in, with no plugin on any device. Every view is named for exactly what it shows.

Three levels

Area, question, answer

A Base's first word is the area you come with: Family (what to watch together), Collection (what we have), Person (my own list). The Base is the question; each view is one answer, named for how it slices it. Nine Bases and 137 views today, none more than one menu away.

Three ticks each

Seen it, I wanna see it, wait for me

Everyone has their own Base and their own checklist, by films, series or everything. A box never ticked shows as a dash: not answered yet, never a silent no. A tick on a phone syncs back to everyone.

Who's watching

The family's own groups

One rule for every group (it suits the youngest in the room, nobody in it said no, nobody outside it said wait for me), plus each group's own. Very highly rated always means IMDb 8+ or Rotten Tomatoes 90%+, and the view's name says so.

Watching together

Content limits, from Common Sense

Common Sense Media's own scores for sex and violence decide what suits the room, rather than the age rating alone: 535 notes have them so far, and until a film has its scores, the age rating stands in.

One click out

Every registry that lists it

IMDb, TMDb, Wikidata, Wikipedia, Letterboxd, Rotten Tomatoes, Metacritic, Common Sense Media and where to watch, as links in each note: 730 notes have them so far, and every new save gets them. A link is built only from an id a source gave, never guessed from a title.

12 · The poster check

A cover on every card, and nothing explicit.

The film cards now show their posters. Because the vault goes to every phone in the house, children's included, no image is published until a filter has looked at it, and the filter runs entirely at home: no paid AI, and no image sent anywhere to be judged. The whole library is done; new saves get theirs soon.

1 · Fetch

Enrich takes the title's one poster from TMDb, at the smallest size still sharp on a phone (about 60 KB), and keeps it in the raw archive.

2 · NudeNet, every poster

An open-source nudity detector, run on the CPU at home. Any exposed-nudity label at confidence 0.5 or above flags the poster; the rest pass.

3 · A second look, flagged only

A vision model on the home network's model box (qwen3-vl) is asked one fixed question: is this sexually explicit, or does it show nudity? Yes or no.

4 · Published, or never

Only a clear no publishes it. Anything else is blocked and stays in the archive, outside every vault. If the model box is down, flagged posters simply wait.

The first run, over the whole library: 729 posters checked and published; NudeNet flagged 2, the vision model cleared both, none was blocked, and no paid AI was called. Every note records how its poster was judged: poster_check is passed, blocked or unchecked, and poster_checked_with names the tools and their versions. Nothing here was invented: NudeNet is the standard open detector, and the vision model was already on the model box.

13 · The first rule
“Leave blanks blank. Do not ever make up data.”The family's own specification, October 2026

Every field in a note holds what a public source said or what a person typed, under a name that says which source it came from: csm_age is Common Sense Media's age, omdb_rotten_tomatoes is Rotten Tomatoes' score as OMDb reported it. When two sources disagree, both stay. When no source gave a value, the field stays empty.

And every value can prove where it came from: the note records which saved response it was read from, and where inside that response. Nothing is ever deleted, so any note can be rebuilt from the raw data, years later.
